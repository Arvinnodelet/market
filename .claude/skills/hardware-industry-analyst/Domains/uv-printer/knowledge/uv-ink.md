# UV Ink Knowledge

## Definition

UV ink is a UV-curable inkjet ink. Unlike conventional solvent or water-based inks, the deposited film is primarily cured through photochemical polymerization initiated by UV exposure.

## Typical Components

```text
UV Ink
├── Pigment
├── Monomer
├── Oligomer / Resin
├── Photoinitiator
└── Additives
```

Actual formulation varies by supplier, printhead, substrate, curing system and application.

## Important Properties

| Property | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Viscosity | Jetting stability |
| Surface tension | Droplet formation / wetting |
| Pigment dispersion | Nozzle reliability / color |
| Photoinitiator system | Curing response |
| Adhesion | Substrate compatibility |
| Flexibility | Crack resistance |
| Cure speed | Printing throughput |
| Odor / migration | Application suitability |

## White Ink

White ink generally has higher pigment loading and therefore creates additional challenges in sedimentation, circulation, filtration and nozzle reliability.

Research questions:

- Is white ink circulated continuously?
- Is the tank agitated?
- Is there temperature control?
- What is the purge / maintenance procedure?
- Is white ink stored separately?

## Ink / Printhead Compatibility

Ink selection cannot be treated independently from the printhead. Important variables include viscosity, surface tension, temperature, waveform and chemical compatibility with wetted components.

## Curing

```text
Ink droplet
    ↓
UV exposure
    ↓
Photoinitiator activation
    ↓
Free-radical / cationic polymerization
    ↓
Crosslinked polymer film
```

Under-cure may cause poor surface properties or adhesion; over-cure can change appearance and material properties. Actual performance depends on formulation, wavelength, irradiance and dose.

## Research Rule

Do not infer a complete ink formulation from the product name alone. Unless a technical data sheet or manufacturer disclosure exists, record formulation details as unknown.
