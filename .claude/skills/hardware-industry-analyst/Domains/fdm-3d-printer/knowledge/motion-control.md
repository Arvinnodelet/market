# Motion Control Knowledge Base

## Purpose

This document summarizes motion control technologies used in modern FDM additive manufacturing systems.

The focus is motion planning, vibration control, extrusion synchronization, and closed-loop optimization.

---

# Motion Planning

## Purpose

Convert toolpaths generated by slicers into executable motor trajectories.

## Responsibilities

* Path interpolation
* Velocity planning
* Acceleration control
* Corner transition handling
* Step generation

## Importance

Motion planning determines:

* Print speed
* Surface quality
* Mechanical stress
* Noise levels

---

# Trapezoidal Velocity Profile

## Principle

Acceleration remains constant until target speed is reached.

Velocity profile:

```text
Accelerate
→ Constant Velocity
→ Decelerate
```

## Advantages

* Simple implementation
* Low computational cost
* Widely adopted

## Disadvantages

* Sudden acceleration changes
* Generates vibration
* More ringing artifacts

## Typical Systems

* Legacy Marlin systems
* Industrial CNC controllers

---

# S-Curve Motion Planning

## Principle

Smooths acceleration transitions by limiting jerk.

Velocity profile:

```text
Acceleration Ramp-Up
→ Constant Acceleration
→ Ramp-Down
```

## Advantages

* Reduced vibration
* Smoother movement
* Better print quality

## Disadvantages

* More computation
* Harder tuning

## Industry Adoption

Common in:

* Klipper
* Bambu Lab
* Industrial motion platforms

---

# Junction Deviation

## Principle

Controls cornering speed while maintaining trajectory accuracy.

Instead of stopping at corners, the planner allows controlled path deviation.

## Advantages

* Higher average speed
* Reduced print time
* Better motion continuity

## Disadvantages

* Excessive values reduce dimensional accuracy

## Evaluation

Performance Impact: High

Complexity: Medium

Industry Adoption: High

---

# Input Shaping

## Principle

Suppresses mechanical resonance through signal pre-processing.

The controller modifies acceleration commands before sending them to motors.

## Purpose

Reduce:

* Ringing
* Ghosting
* Mechanical oscillation

## Advantages

* Enables higher acceleration
* Improves surface quality
* No mechanical redesign required

## Disadvantages

* Requires resonance measurement
* Incorrect tuning reduces effectiveness

## Typical Systems

* Klipper
* Bambu Lab
* Voron ecosystem

## Evaluation

Performance Impact: Very High

Complexity: Medium

Industry Adoption: Very High

---

# Resonance Compensation

## Principle

Measures machine resonant frequencies and applies compensation.

## Sensor Sources

* Accelerometers
* Vibration sensors
* Sensor fusion systems

## Advantages

* Enables high-speed printing
* Improves dimensional consistency

## Disadvantages

* Additional hardware
* Calibration requirements

## Industry Trend

Rapidly becoming standard on premium printers.

---

# Pressure Advance

## Principle

Compensates for pressure buildup inside the nozzle during speed changes.

Extrusion is adjusted before motion changes occur.

## Benefits

* Sharper corners
* Improved dimensional accuracy
* Reduced over-extrusion

## Challenges

* Material dependent
* Requires tuning

## Typical Systems

* Klipper
* Modern proprietary controllers

## Evaluation

Print Quality Impact: Very High

Complexity: Medium

Industry Adoption: High

---

# Sensorless Homing

## Principle

Uses motor back-EMF and driver feedback to detect end-of-travel.

No mechanical limit switch required.

## Advantages

* Lower BOM cost
* Simplified wiring
* Cleaner assembly

## Disadvantages

* Less repeatable than precision probes
* Sensitive to motor tuning

## Typical Drivers

* TMC2209
* TMC2240
* TMC5160

---

# Closed-Loop Stepper Control

## Principle

Uses encoder feedback to verify actual motor position.

Controller continuously compares:

```text
Command Position
vs
Actual Position
```

## Advantages

* Detects missed steps
* Improves reliability
* Enables industrial applications

## Disadvantages

* Higher BOM cost
* Additional electronics
* More software complexity

## Typical Applications

* Industrial printers
* High-end automation systems

---

# Motion Controller Architectures

## MCU-Centric

### Examples

* Marlin
* RepRap derivatives

### Characteristics

* Motion planning inside MCU
* Lower hardware cost
* Limited compute resources

---

## Linux + MCU

### Examples

* Klipper
* Bambu Lab
* Modern high-speed systems

### Characteristics

* Motion planning on Linux processor
* Real-time execution on MCU
* Supports advanced algorithms

### Advantages

* Higher computational capability
* Easier software evolution
* Better support for AI and vision systems
